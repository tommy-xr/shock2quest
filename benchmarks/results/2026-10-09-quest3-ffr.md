# Quest 3 fixed foveation: Many brain crowd

FFR reduces VrApi App time, but **this sweep does not show a fresh-frame win or stable 90/120 Hz**. High saves 0.653 ms (7.5%) versus off on average; medium saves 0.516 ms. CPU update remains around 6.3 ms, and later runs develop substantially more scene/eye wall time and stale frames. Keep FFR opt-in and investigate the warm-run regression before selecting a default.

## Conditions

- Quest 3, Android 14; release APK SHA-256 `36af1c509266ccd5c390132123ceed7c04493ba6e0fc25ed30af4580ccb7491e`.
- Source: `30bece19` (built from the equivalent working tree after `d32664c4`, before committing the profile-verification fix). FFR off by default. CPU/clock diagnostics from #2126 are not included in this APK.
- 1680×1760 per eye; active refresh verified at 90 Hz throughout all measured intervals. One identical APK and asset set across all 16 runs.
- `many-brain-mixed-crowd`; upgraded terrain on, wetness 0, object lighting on. Same camera, spotlight, 4 rumbler meshes, 6 overlord meshes, 8 egg meshes; 6 live animation subjects verified each second.
- 10 s warmup + 30 s timing per run; GPU counters collected for a separate 10 s after timing/capture. Four balanced Latin-square orders: off/low/high/medium; low/medium/off/high; medium/high/low/off; high/off/medium/low.
- Normal device governors, externally powered; no global foveation override. GPU clock was 599 MHz in off repeat 1 and 545 MHz in the other timing intervals. Temperatures rose through the sweep; compare repetitions, not only means. No forced clock/thermal setting was changed.
- Both eye profile updates must succeed; each workload record confirms requested/effective level. App-PID VrApi independently confirms Fov 0/1/2/3 with no dynamic suffix.

## Warm-run regression needs isolation

The first six timing intervals deliver 87.6–88.6 FPS with scene preparation
around 0.7 ms. From off repeat 2 onward, delivery falls to 78.6–82.8 FPS,
scene preparation rises to roughly 1.1–1.3 ms, and both-eye wall time rises.
Temperature increases from a 36.6°C mean in the first run to 50°C in the last.
The transition also follows the first invalid hardware-counter interval.
No `ovrgpuprofiler` process remained after the sweep, but the cause is **not
isolated**: thermal/governor behavior, driver state, and profiler interference
must be distinguished with a timing-only repeat and CPU attribution.

Counterbalancing does not erase this regime change. High's aggregate FPS is
lower than off despite its lower App time; do not present the GPU headroom as
an overall speedup. One-second telemetry cannot establish the 8.33 ms p99 gate.

## Hardware counters: limited valid evidence

Only five of the sixteen hardware-counter intervals were valid: off-1, low-1, high-1, medium-1, low-2. Every later interval returned an unavailable `Fragments Shaded / Second = -1` sentinel and is excluded **in full** from hardware-counter summaries. This is not evidence of zero or negative GPU work. The separate timing intervals remain valid; raw invalid counters are retained.

## Visuals and decision

Both eyes render the intended workload at all four levels without gross corruption or missing geometry. The fixed-camera compositor captures do not show a confidently obvious peripheral-quality loss, but the dark scene, live animation differences, capture resampling and lenses limit that comparison. The GIF alternates four stills; it is not motion footage. Subsequent MedSci headset review found high noticeably blurry when looking away from center, off much clearer, and low a minor difference that remained readable. The user approved low as the default; medium was not wearer-tested.

Low/medium/high save approximately 0.441/0.516/0.653 ms of App time. The
incremental savings between levels are small relative to the CPU budget and
warm-run variation. Select low as the default based on the measured App-time
saving and acceptable wearer feedback, retaining an explicit off override.
This is a quality/performance tradeoff, not proof of improved sustained FPS;
a timing-only thermally settled run remains necessary. Next: attribute CPU phases and actual solver
time, confirm the candidates in Rec/MedSci, then correct the simulation clock
before raising refresh. Stable 120 Hz still requires per-frame tail/freshness
evidence and interaction validation.

## Reproduction and provenance

```sh
node tools/quest-bench/run.mjs --scene many-brain-mixed-crowd \
  --lighting on --terrain upgraded --ffr all --repeats 4 \
  --warmup 10 --seconds 30 --gpu-seconds 10 --output /tmp/quest-ffr
```

The first short pilot safely fell back to off after an invalid profile-readback assumption and was rejected. The corrected pilot verified fixed low FFR before this matrix. The original sweep exits nonzero because unavailable hardware counters mark a run failed; archived results additionally separate `timing_status=ok` from `gpu_status=invalid` so failures are not hidden or valid timing discarded. Device serials are redacted. Assets hashes, fixtures, per-run telemetry, startup markers, batteries and counter mapping are in the [raw archive](2026-10-09-quest3-ffr/).

Telemetry correction: `Fov=3D` means dynamic level 3, not another display. This sweep filters by application PID and requires the fixed level. See [Meta FFR documentation](https://developers.meta.com/vr/documentation/native/android/os-fixed-foveated-rendering/).
